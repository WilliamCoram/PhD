## 3.8.1 (continued), p. 171

"In order to be able to transform inequality (b) into an equality — which would allow us to compute
| |_sup on A in terms of | |_sup on the smaller algebra B — we must impose some additional
assumptions on A and B. We say that the *Maximum Modulus Principle* holds for a k-algebra A if, for
all f ∈ A, there exists an x ∈ Max_k A such that |f(x)| = |f|_sup."

**Proposition 7 (3.8.1/7), p. 171.** "Let φ: B → A be an integral torsion-free k-algebra
monomorphism between two k-algebras A and B, where B is an integrally closed (integral) domain.
Then one has
(a) |f|_sup = max_{1≤i≤n} |b_i|_sup^{1/i} for f ∈ A, where fⁿ + φ(b₁) fⁿ⁻¹ + ⋯ + φ(bₙ) = 0 is the
(unique) integral equation of minimal degree for f over φ(B).
(b) The Maximum Modulus Principle holds for A if and only if it holds for B.
(c) | |_sup is a norm on A if and only if | |_sup is a norm on B and A is reduced.
(d) We have |φ(b) f|_sup = |b|_sup |f|_sup for all b ∈ B and all f ∈ A if and only if
|bb'|_sup = |b|_sup |b'|_sup for all b, b' ∈ B."

*Preliminaries (pp. 171–172).* "Before proving the proposition, let us recall briefly how, for a
given element f ∈ A, its integral equation of minimal degree over φ(B) is obtained. We may assume
that B is a subalgebra of A and that A = B[f]. Let Q(B) be the field of fractions of B and define
Q(A) := A_{B−{0}}. Since A is torsion-free over B, we have a commutative diagram of inclusions
B ⊂ A, Q(B) ⊂ Q(A). The canonical epimorphism τ: B[X] → A, X ↦ f, extends to an epimorphism
τ': Q(B)[X] → Q(A), and since Q(B) is a field, there is a unique monic polynomial q ∈ Q(B)[X]
generating the ideal ker τ'. We claim that q has, in fact, coefficients in B. Assuming this for the
moment, we can easily see that q(f) = 0 is the unique integral equation of minimal degree for f
over B. Namely, we show that the ideal ker τ = B[X] ∩ ker τ' is generated by q. Let p be any
polynomial in ker τ. Using Euclid's division for q ∈ B[X], we have p = q₁q + r, where
q₁, r ∈ B[X] and deg r < deg q unless r = 0. Since q ∈ ker τ, we must have r ∈ ker τ ⊂ ker τ'.
However this is only possible if r = 0. Thus we see that ker τ is generated by q and that τ
induces an isomorphism B[X]/(q) ≅ A.

It remains to be shown that the polynomial q has coefficients in B. Since q generates the kernel of
the epimorphism τ': Q(B)[X] → Q(A), X ↦ f, we have an isomorphism Q(B)[X]/(q) ≅ Q(A). Let us first
consider the case where q is irreducible in Q(B)[X]. Then Q(A) is a field, namely the field
generated by f over Q(B), and q is the corresponding minimal polynomial. Let K be the splitting
field of q over Q(B) (where Q(B) ⊂ Q(A) ⊂ K). Then K contains all conjugates of f over Q(B). Since
f is integral over B so are its conjugates. Thus the coefficients of q (being the elementary
symmetric polynomials in the conjugates of f) must be integral over B so that q ∈ B[X] because B is
integrally closed in its field of fractions Q(B).

If q is not irreducible, we consider a decomposition into prime factors in Q(B)[X], say
q = p₁ ⋯ p_r. Then, for each i, one can consider Q(B)[X]/(p_i) as a finite extension of Q(B).
Concluding as before, one sees that p_i ∈ B[X] for all i. Thus we have q ∈ B[X] also in this case."

*Proof of Proposition 7, Ad (a) (p. 172).* "We may assume that B is a subalgebra of A and that
A = B[f]. Let q(f) = 0 be the integral equation of minimal degree for f over B, where
q = Xⁿ + b₁Xⁿ⁻¹ + ⋯ + bₙ ∈ B[X]. Then we have A = B[f] ≅ B[X]/(q) by the above considerations
(where f corresponds to the residue class of X in B[X]/(q)). For y ∈ Max_k B, denote by f_y the
residue class of f in A/yA and by f̄_y its residue class in red A/yA (the quotient of A/yA modulo
its nilradical). Similarly, let q_y be the residue class of q in (B/y)[X]. Since each y ∈ Max_k B
is contained in some ideal x ∈ Max_k A, we see that
    |f|_sup = sup_{y ∈ Max_k B} |f_y|_sup = sup_{y ∈ Max_k B} |f̄_y|_sup.
For a fixed y ∈ Max_k B, let q_y = q₁^{n₁} ⋯ q_r^{n_r} be the decomposition of q_y into prime
factors, where q₁, …, q_r are pairwise different monic polynomials in (B/y)[X]. Then
    A/yA = (B/y)[X]/(q_y)
and
    red (A/yA) = (B/y)[X]/(q₁ ⋯ q_r) = ⊕_{ν=1}^r (B/y)[X]/(q_ν).
Provide B/y with the spectral norm over k and denote by | |_ν the spectral norm on (B/y)[X]/(q_ν)
over B/y (which equals the spectral norm over k by Proposition 3.2.2/4). Then if f̄_ν is the residue
class of f̄_y in (B/y)[X]/(q_ν), we have by Corollary 3.2.1/6 (cf. also the remark following Lemma
3.8.1/5)
    |f̄_y|_sup = max_{1≤ν≤r} |f̄_ν|_ν = max_{1≤ν≤r} σ(q_ν) = σ(q_y).
Therefore
    |f|_sup = sup_{y ∈ Max_k B} σ(q_y) = sup_{y ∈ Max_k B} max_{1≤i≤n} |b_i(y)|^{1/i}
            = max_{1≤i≤n} |b_i|_sup^{1/i},
which verifies assertion (a)."

*Ad (b) (p. 172).* "If the Maximum Modulus Principle holds for B, then (using the same notations as
before) there is a y ∈ Max_k B such that |f̄_y|_sup = σ(q_y) = |f|_sup. Since A/yA contains only
finitely many maximal ideals, there must exist an x ∈ Max_k A such that |f̄_y|_sup = |f(x)|. Hence
the Maximum Modulus Principle holds for A. The converse follows from Lemma 6 (a)."

*Ad (c) (pp. 172–173).* "Assume that A is reduced and that | |_sup is a norm on B, i.e., | |_sup
is finite and |b|_sup = 0 implies b = 0 for all b ∈ B. We have to show that | |_sup is a norm on A.
Take f ∈ A with f ≠ 0, and let fⁿ + b₁fⁿ⁻¹ + ⋯ + bₙ = 0 be the integral equation of minimal degree
for f over B. Because A is reduced, there exists an index m with 1 ≤ m ≤ n such that b_m ≠ 0. By
assumption we have |b_m|_sup ≠ 0; hence
    |f|_sup = max_{1≤i≤n} |b_i|_sup^{1/i} ≥ |b_m|_sup^{1/m} > 0.
Using assertion (c) of Lemma 6, we conclude that | |_sup is a norm on A. Since any ring with a
power-multiplicative norm is reduced, the converse follows from (a) and (c) of Lemma 6."

*Ad (d) (p. 173).* "Consider an element f ∈ A, and let fⁿ + b₁fⁿ⁻¹ + ⋯ + bₙ = 0 be the corresponding
integral equation of minimal degree over B. Then f is of degree n over the fraction field Q(B), and
so is any product bf where b ∈ B − {0}. Therefore
    (bf)ⁿ + bb₁(bf)ⁿ⁻¹ + ⋯ + bⁿbₙ = 0
is the integral equation of minimal degree for any such product bf. Now assume that | |_sup is
multiplicative on B, i.e., that |bb'|_sup = |b|_sup |b'|_sup for all b, b' ∈ B. Then we can compute
|bf|_sup according to (a):
    |bf|_sup = max_{1≤i≤n} |b^i b_i|_sup^{1/i} = |b|_sup max_{1≤i≤n} |b_i|_sup^{1/i} = |b|_sup |f|_sup.
Since this equation is trivially true for b = 0, we have verified the if part of assertion (d). The
only if part follows immediately from assertion (a) of Lemma 6. □"

**Corollary 8 (3.8.1/8), p. 173.** "In addition to the assumptions of Proposition 7, assume that the
Maximum Modulus Principle holds for B. Then for every f ∈ A with |f| ≠ 0, there exist c ∈ k and
m ∈ ℕ such that |cf^m|_sup = 1."

*Proof.* "For all b ∈ B, there exists y ∈ Max_k B such that |b|_sup = |b(y)|. Hence |B|_sup ⊂ |k_a|.
The set |k_a| is invariant under the operation of taking roots. Therefore
{|b|_sup^{1/i}; b ∈ B − {0} and i ∈ ℕ} ⊂ |k_a|. According to assertion (a) of the preceding
proposition, |f|_sup ∈ |k_a| for all f ∈ A. Hence there are an element d ∈ k and an integer m such
that |d|^{1/m} = |f|_sup. Define c := d⁻¹ ∈ k. Then 1 = |cf^m|_sup. □"

**Lemma 9 (3.8.1/9), p. 173.** "If | |_sup is a norm on a k-algebra A, then ⋂_{𝔪 ∈ Max_k A} 𝔪 = (0).
In particular, A is reduced, and the Jacobson radical ⋂_{𝔪 ∈ Max A} 𝔪 vanishes." ("For later
reference we state the following trivial fact.")

**Remark (pp. 173–174).** "Assume that | |_sup is a valuation on B. Then it can be extended to a
valuation on the field of fractions Q(B). If we assume furthermore that A is reduced, then
Q(A) := A_{φ(B)−{0}} is a reduced integral Q(B)-algebra. According to assertion (d) of Proposition
7, the function | |_sup defines a faithful B-module norm on A; hence it can be extended to a
Q(B)-algebra norm on Q(A). According to assertion (a) of Proposition 7, this norm is nothing more
than the spectral norm on the integral Q(B)-algebra Q(A), as defined in (3.2.2). To put it in
another way: *The spectral norm on Q(A) considered as a Q(B)-algebra yields — if restricted to A —
the supremum norm on A considered as a k-algebra.*"

## 3.8.2 (continued), pp. 175–178

**Proposition 3 (3.8.2/3), p. 175.** "Let A be a k-Banach algebra. Assume that | |_sup is a norm on
A. Then every k-algebra homomorphism φ from an arbitrary k-Banach algebra B into A is continuous."

*Proof (p. 176).* "For all x ∈ Max_k A, consider the commutative diagram
    B —φ→ A,  β: B → B/φ⁻¹(x),  α: A → A/x,  φ̄: B/φ⁻¹(x) → A/x,  ψ = φ̄ ∘ β = α ∘ φ,
where α and β are the canonical epimorphisms. Because A/x is algebraic over k, so is B/φ⁻¹(x).
Hence φ⁻¹(x) ∈ Max_k B. Provide A/x and B/φ⁻¹(x) with the spectral norm. The map φ̄ is then an
isometry. The epimorphisms α and β are contractions according to Lemma 1. Thus we know that
ψ = φ̄ ∘ β is continuous. Then by Lemma 3.8.1/9, we may finish the proof by using the Closed Graph
Theorem (in almost literally the same way as in the proof of Proposition 3.7.5/1). □"

**Corollary 4 (3.8.2/4), p. 176.** "If A is a k-Banach algebra such that | |_sup is a norm on A,
then all complete k-algebra norms on A are equivalent."

"In Corollary 2 we only have an estimate for | |_sup. Now we want to compute | |_sup in terms of
| |. This can be done if we add topological conditions to the assumptions of Proposition 3.8.1/7."

**Proposition 5 (3.8.2/5), p. 176.** "Let φ: B → A be an integral torsion-free k-algebra
monomorphism between two k-algebras A and B, where B is an integrally closed domain. Assume
furthermore that A is a k-Banach algebra with norm | | and that φ is continuous if B is provided
with the topology induced by | |_sup. Then one has for all f ∈ A
    inf_{i ∈ ℕ} |f^i|^{1/i} = |f|_sup = max_{1≤i≤n} |b_i|_sup^{1/i},
where fⁿ + φ(b₁) fⁿ⁻¹ + ⋯ + φ(bₙ) = 0 is the integral equation of minimal degree for f over φ(B)."

*Proof.* "We only have to show that |f|_r = |f|_sup, where |f|_r := inf_{i ∈ ℕ} |f^i|^{1/i}. From
Corollary 2 one deduces immediately that |f|_sup ≤ |f|_r. In order to show the opposite
inequality, we shall use Proposition 3.8.1/7. Since | |_r is a power-multiplicative semi-norm on
A, one can apply Proposition 3.1.2/1 to A/ker | |_r (viewed as a normed algebra over itself), and
one gets
    |f|_r ≤ max_{1≤i≤n} |φ(b_i)|_r^{1/i} ≤ max_{1≤i≤n} |φ(b_i)|^{1/i}.
Because φ is continuous, there is a real constant C > 1 such that |φ(b)| ≤ C|b|_sup for all b ∈ B,
and a fortiori |φ(b)|^{1/i} ≤ C|b|_sup^{1/i} for all i ∈ ℕ. Thus by Proposition 3.8.1/7, we have
shown that |f|_r ≤ C max_{1≤i≤n} |b_i|_sup^{1/i} = C|f|_sup. This implies |f|_r ≤ |f|_sup, since
both | |_r and | |_sup are power-multiplicative. □"

**Remark 1 (pp. 176–177).** "In spite of the fact that | |_sup depends only on the algebraic
structure of the k-algebra A (and, of course, on the valuation on k), | |_sup nevertheless
coincides with the norm | |_r which is derived from the given Banach norm on A. This is a result
similar to Corollary 4, asserting that (roughly speaking) the topological structure of the k-Banach
algebra A is already determined by the underlying algebraic structure."

**Remark 2 (p. 177).** "The assumption 'φ is continuous' of Proposition 5 is automatically fulfilled
if A is reduced and if | |_sup is a complete norm on B. Namely, B provided with | |_sup is then a
k-Banach algebra and | |_sup is a norm on A by Proposition 3.8.1/7 (c). Applying Proposition 3, we
see that φ is continuous."

p. 177: "We want to conclude this section by looking at topologically nilpotent and power-bounded
elements of a k-Banach algebra A. Since inf_{i ∈ ℕ} |f^i|^{1/i} = lim_{i→∞} |f^i|^{1/i} for any
semi-norm (cf. (1.3.2)), it is easily seen that an element f is topologically nilpotent in A if and
only if inf |f^i|^{1/i} < 1. Furthermore, we have inf |f^i|^{1/i} ≤ 1 if f is power-bounded. In the
special situation described in Proposition 5, the latter implication is, in fact, an equivalence.
Namely,"

**Corollary 6 (3.8.2/6), p. 177.** "Under the hypotheses of Proposition 5, the following statements
are equivalent for all f ∈ A:
(a) f is topologically nilpotent, (b) inf_{i ∈ ℕ} |f^i|^{1/i} < 1, (c) |f|_sup < 1.
If the Maximum Modulus Principle holds for A or B, then (a), (b) and (c) are equivalent to
(d) |f(x)| < 1 for all x ∈ Max_k A.
Furthermore, the following statements are equivalent for all f ∈ A:
(a') f is power-bounded, (b') inf_{i ∈ ℕ} |f^i|^{1/i} ≤ 1, (c') |f|_sup ≤ 1."

*Proof (pp. 177–178).* "It has already been mentioned that (a) and (b) are equivalent. The preceding
proposition yields the equivalence of (b) and (c). Clearly (c) and (d) are equivalent if the
Maximum Modulus Principle holds for A. Furthermore by Proposition 3.8.1/7 (b), if the Maximum
Modulus Principle holds for B, it holds also for A.

As already indicated, (a') implies (b'). The equivalence of (b') and (c') follows from Proposition
5. Hence it suffices to show that (c') implies (a'). Use the notations of Proposition 5. Then from
(c') we get |b_i|_sup ≤ 1 for i = 1, …, n, and therefore |b|_sup ≤ 1 for all b ∈ P[b₁, …, bₙ],
where P is the prime ring of B, i.e., the smallest subring of B containing 1. Since φ is
continuous, R := φ(P)[φ(b₁), …, φ(bₙ)] is bounded under | |. From the integral equation for f, one
easily derives f^j ∈ Σ_{i=0}^{n−1} R f^i for all j ∈ ℕ (use induction on j). Hence f is
power-bounded with respect to | |. □"

## 3.8.3 Banach function algebras (pp. 178–182)

"The main result of the preceding section (Proposition 3.8.2/5) allows us to compute | |_sup on A
in terms of the given complete norm on A, but it does not answer the question of whether or not
| |_sup also induces the Banach topology on A. We shall attack this problem from a somewhat more
general point of view and start with the following definition:"

**Definition 1 (3.8.3/1).** "A k-algebra A is called a Banach function algebra if | |_sup is a
complete norm on A."

**Lemma 2 (3.8.3/2).** "A k-Banach algebra A is a Banach function algebra if and only if | |_sup is
equivalent to the given norm on A."
*Proof.* "The if part is obvious. The only if part follows from Corollary 3.8.2/4. □"

**Lemma 3 (3.8.3/3).** "If A is a Banach function algebra, then | |_sup is the only
power-multiplicative complete k-algebra norm on A."
*Proof.* "Since any two power-multiplicative k-algebra norms inducing the same topology must
coincide (cf. Proposition 3.1.5/1), there is at most one power-multiplicative complete k-algebra
norm on A by Lemma 2. On the other hand, | |_sup is power-multiplicative and complete if A is a
Banach function algebra. □"

**Lemma 4 (3.8.3/4), p. 178.** "Let φ: B → A be a k-algebra monomorphism between two k-algebras A
and B. Assume that A is a Banach function algebra. Then B is a Banach function algebra if φ(B) is
closed in A. In particular, a closed subalgebra of a Banach function algebra is again a Banach
function algebra."
*Proof (p. 179).* "Let | |_sup^(A) (resp. | |_sup^(B)) denote the supremum semi-norm on A (resp.
B). The algebra φ(B), provided with the restriction of | |_sup^(A), is a k-Banach subalgebra of A,
because A is a Banach function algebra and because φ(B) is closed in A. Lifting this Banach algebra
structure back to B by means of φ (i.e., by defining |b| := |φ(b)|_sup^(A) for b ∈ B), we get a
k-Banach algebra structure on B. Applying Corollary 3.8.2/2, we see that the new norm dominates the
supremum semi-norm on B — i.e., |b|_sup^(B) ≤ |b| for all b ∈ B. On the other hand, by Lemma
3.8.1/4, the monomorphism φ is a contraction with respect to the supremum semi-norms, i.e.,
|b| = |φ(b)|_sup^(A) ≤ |b|_sup^(B) for all b ∈ B. Putting these inequalities together, we see that
| |_sup^(B) coincides with the complete norm | |. □"

**Remark.** "If, in addition to the assumptions of Lemma 4, the monomorphism φ is supposed to be
integral, then the closedness of φ(B) is also necessary for B to be a Banach function algebra. This
follows from Lemma 3.8.1/6 (a)."

**Corollary 5 (3.8.3/5), p. 179.** "Let φ: B → A be a finite k-algebra monomorphism, where B is a
Noetherian k-Banach algebra and A is a Banach function algebra. Then B is a Banach function
algebra."
*Proof.* "Provide A with the norm | |_sup. By Proposition 3.8.2/3, the map φ is continuous. For all
b ∈ B, we have |φ(b)|_sup ≤ |b|_sup by Lemma 3.8.1/4 and |b|_sup ≤ |b| by Corollary 3.8.2/2.
Therefore we can view A as a finite normed B-module. Since B is a Noetherian k-Banach algebra, all
B-submodules of A are closed (see Proposition 3.7.2/2); in particular, φ(B) is closed in A. Now the
assertion follows from the preceding lemma. □"

**Proposition 6 (3.8.3/6), p. 179.** "Let char k = 0, and let φ: B → A be a finite torsion-free
k-algebra monomorphism, where B is a Noetherian integrally closed domain and where A is reduced.
Then A is a Banach function algebra if B is a Banach function algebra."
*Proof (pp. 179–181).* "First we want to show that B̊ = {b ∈ B; |b|_sup ≤ 1} is integrally closed in
its field of fractions. Let f, g be elements in B̊ with g ≠ 0 such that u := f/g is integral over
B̊. Since B̊ and B have the same field of fractions Q(B) and since B is integrally closed in Q(B),
we know that, in fact, u ∈ B. Let u^m + b₁u^{m−1} + ⋯ + b_m = 0 be an integral equation for u over
B̊. Then we have by Proposition 3.1.2/1
    |u|_sup ≤ max |b_i|_sup^{1/i} ≤ 1.
Hence u ∈ B̊, and B̊ is integrally closed. Similarly, using Proposition 3.8.1/7 (a), we see that Å
is integral over B̊.
Let us now look at the ring of fractions Q(A) := A_{φ(B)−{0}}, which is a finite Q(B)-algebra. We
want to show that there exists a Q(B)-basis v₁, …, v_r of Q(A) over Q(B) such that
Å ⊂ Σ_{i=1}^r B̊ v_i. First we consider the case, where A is an integral domain, or equivalently,
where Q(A) is a field. Since we are working in characteristic 0, we know that Q(A) is a separable
extension of Q(B) of degree r < ∞, say generated by an element a ∈ Q(A). We may assume that the
minimal polynomial of a has coefficients in B̊ so that a is integral over B̊. Let a = a₁, …, a_r be
the conjugates of a over Q(B) and set K := Q(B)(a₁, …, a_r). Then any f ∈ Q(A) can be written as
f = Σ_{i=0}^{r−1} b_i a^i with coefficients b_i ∈ Q(B). Using Galois automorphisms over Q(B) on K,
we get equations f_j = Σ_{i=0}^{r−1} b_i a_j^i, j = 1, …, r, where f_j is the image of f under the
automorphism mapping a to a_j. If one views these as a system of linear equations in the unknown
b_i, the determinant of the coefficient matrix is Vandermonde's determinant
d = det(a_j^i) = ∏_{ν>μ} (a_ν − a_μ). Since d² (the discriminant of the minimal polynomial of a) is
invariant under Galois automorphisms, we have d² ∈ Q(B). (In fact, d² ∈ B̊ since all a_j and hence
d² are integral over B̊.) If f is integral over B̊, then all f_j are integral over B̊. Therefore
Cramer's rule shows that in this case all elements d²b_i are integral over B̊; hence they are
contained in B̊, because, as we saw, B̊ is integrally closed. Thus we have verified that the
integral closure of B̊ in Q(A) (and, in particular, Å) is contained in Σ_{i=0}^{r−1} B̊(d⁻²a^i).
If Q(A) is not a field, it is a finite direct sum of finite extensions L₁, …, L_t of Q(B) (use
Dedekind's Lemma 3.1.4/1). Then the above consideration can be carried out for each extension L_i
separately, and the desired result follows.
Let v₁, …, v_r be a Q(B)-basis of Q(A) such that Å ⊂ Σ_{i=1}^r B̊ v_i, and choose an element b ≠ 0
in B̊ such that all products v_{i₁}v_{i₂} are contained in Σ_{i=1}^r B̊ b⁻¹ v_i. Then
Å ⊂ Σ B̊ v_i ⊂ Σ B̊ b⁻¹ v_i and, in particular, A ⊂ Σ B v_i ⊂ F := Σ B b⁻¹ v_i.
We assume that B is a k-Banach algebra under | |_sup and consider a norm | | on the finite
B-module F, which is given by |Σ_{i=1}^r b_i v_i/b| := max_{1≤i≤r} |b_i|_sup.
Then F is a complete Noetherian B-module. By Proposition 3.7.2/2, all submodules of F are closed.
In particular, A is closed in F and therefore a k-Banach space under | |. An easy verification
shows that the multiplication is continuous in A (due to the choice of the element b ∈ B̊). Hence
by Proposition 1.2.1/2, we can define a k-algebra norm ‖ ‖ on A by ‖f‖ := sup_{g ∈ A−{0}} |fg|/|g|,
which is equivalent to | |.
We want to conclude the proof by showing that | |_sup is equivalent to ‖ ‖ and hence to | | on A.
Then it follows that A is a Banach function algebra. We have | |_sup ≤ ‖ ‖ by Corollary 3.8.2/2.
Thus it is enough to find a constant C > 0 such that |f| ≤ C|f|_sup for all f ∈ A. Choose an
element c ∈ k with |c| > 1. We claim that |f| ≤ |c| |f|_sup for all f ∈ A. In order to verify this,
consider a fixed element f ≠ 0 in A. Then |f|_sup ≠ 0 by Proposition 3.8.1/7 (c). Hence there
exists an m ∈ ℤ such that |c|^{m−1} < |f|_sup ≤ |c|^m. Then |c^{−m}f|_sup ≤ 1 and
|c|^m < |c| |f|_sup. Set g := c^{−m}f so that g ∈ Å. Since Å ⊂ Σ_{i=1}^r B̊ b⁻¹v_i, one can find
elements b₁, …, b_r ∈ B̊ such that g = Σ_{i=1}^r b_i(b⁻¹v_i). Then one has
    |f| = |c^m g| = |Σ_{i=1}^r (c^m b_i)(b⁻¹v_i)| = max_{1≤i≤r} |c^m b_i|_sup ≤ |c|^m ≤ |c| |f|_sup,
which finishes our proof. □"

p. 181: "For char k = p > 0, we do not know whether or not Proposition 6 holds without additional
assumptions. At any rate, it remains true for char k = p if A is finite over the k^p-algebra A^p.
Instead of pursuing this further, we shall give another criterion for A to be a Banach function
algebra, which is valid in all characteristics and which is quite adequate for the applications to
k-affinoid algebras in (6.2.2)."

**Theorem 7 (3.8.3/7), p. 181.** "Let φ: B → A be a finite torsion-free k-algebra monomorphism,
where A is reduced and where B is a valued integrally closed Noetherian k-Banach algebra such that
the field of fractions Q(B) is weakly stable (cf. (3.5)). Then A is a Banach function algebra, and
|f|_sup = |f|_sp for all f ∈ A, where | |_sp denotes the spectral norm on the finite-dimensional
reduced Q(B)-algebra Q(A) := A_{φ(B)−{0}}."

*Proof (pp. 181–182).* "Clearly Q(A) is a finite-dimensional reduced Q(B)-algebra and hence a
ring-theoretic direct sum of finitely many finite extensions of Q(B) (see Dedekind's Lemma
3.1.4/1). Since Q(B) is weakly stable, all these extensions are weakly Q(B)-cartesian under their
spectral norm. Then Q(A), provided with its spectral norm, is weakly Q(B)-cartesian (use Theorem
3.2.2/2). Let a₁, …, aₙ be a Q(B)-basis of Q(A). Because φ is finite, there is a universal
denominator b ∈ B − {0} such that A ⊂ A' := Σ_{i=1}^n B a_i/b ⊂ Q(A). Since | |_sp induces the
Q(B)-product topology on Q(A), and since B is complete, we get a complete finite (and hence
Noetherian) B-module if we restrict | |_sp to A'. By Proposition 3.7.2/2, the B-submodule A of A'
is closed with respect to the restriction of | |_sp. Because A' is complete, A is complete and
hence a k-Banach algebra. From Proposition 3.8.1/7 (a), we derive |f|_sup = |f|_sp for all f ∈ A.
Hence | |_sup is a complete norm on A, and therefore A is a Banach function algebra. □"

**Remark (p. 182).** "If A is assumed to be a k-Banach algebra with given norm | |, then by
Corollary 3.8.2/4 and Proposition 3.8.2/5, | |_sup is equivalent to | | and |f|_sup = inf_{i ∈ ℕ}
|f^i|^{1/i}."
