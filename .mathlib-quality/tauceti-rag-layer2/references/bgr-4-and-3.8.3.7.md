## 3.8.3 (end), pp. 181–182 — where weak stability of Q(B) is used (Layer 2, not Layer 0)

"For char k = p > 0, we do not know whether or not Proposition 6 holds without additional
assumptions. At any rate, it remains true for char k = p if A is finite over the k^p-algebra A^p.
Instead of pursuing this further, we shall give another criterion for A to be a Banach function
algebra, which is valid in all characteristics and which is quite adequate for the applications to
k-affinoid algebras in (6.2.2)."

**Theorem 7 (3.8.3/7).** Let φ: B → A be a finite torsion-free k-algebra monomorphism, where A is
reduced and where B is a valued integrally closed Noetherian k-Banach algebra such that the field
of fractions Q(B) is weakly stable (cf. (3.5)). Then A is a Banach function algebra, and
|f|_sup = |f|_sp for all f ∈ A, where | |_sp denotes the spectral norm on the finite-dimensional
reduced Q(B)-algebra Q(A) := A_{φ(B)−{0}}.

*Proof.* Clearly Q(A) is a finite-dimensional reduced Q(B)-algebra and hence a ring-theoretic direct
sum of finitely many finite extensions of Q(B) (see Dedekind's Lemma 3.1.4/1). Since Q(B) is weakly
stable, all these extensions are weakly Q(B)-cartesian under their spectral norm. Then Q(A),
provided with its spectral norm, is weakly Q(B)-cartesian (use Theorem 3.2.2/2). Let a₁, …, aₙ be a
Q(B)-basis of Q(A). Because φ is finite, there is a universal denominator b ∈ B − {0} such that
A ⊂ A' := Σᵢ B·(aᵢ/b) ⊂ Q(A). Since | |_sp induces the Q(B)-product topology on Q(A), and since B is
complete, we get a complete finite (and hence Noetherian) B-module if we restrict | |_sp to A'. By
Proposition 3.7.2/2, the B-submodule A of A' is closed with respect to the restriction of | |_sp.
Because A' is complete, A is complete and hence a k-Banach algebra. From Proposition 3.8.1/7 (a),
we derive |f|_sup = |f|_sp for all f ∈ A. Hence | |_sup is a complete norm on A, and therefore A is
a Banach function algebra. ∎

## Chapter 4. Tame modules and Japanese rings (Appendix to Part A), p. 183

"The purpose of this chapter is to establish some auxiliary results from commutative algebra. By A
we always mean an integral domain (no norm or valuation is required). Let K = Q(A) be the field of
fractions of A. For any A-module M, we denote by Q(M) the K-vector space M ⊗_A K. This is the
localization of M with respect to the multiplicative system A − {0}. By rk_A M we always mean the
*rank* of M, i.e., the maximal number of A-linearly independent elements of M. We have
rk_A M = dim_K Q(M)."

### 4.1 Tame modules, pp. 183–184

"The results of this section are needed with others to derive certain criteria for Japaneseness of
Noetherian integral domains. Each finitely generated A-module M is of finite rank. The converse is
not in general true if A ≠ K; namely in this case, K itself is an A-module of rank 1 which is not
finitely generated."

**Definition 1 (4.1/1).** An A-module M is called *tame* if each A-submodule N ⊂ M of finite rank
is finitely generated.

"Obviously, each such submodule N is then Noetherian (i.e., all submodules of N are finitely
generated). *Thus M is tame if and only if each submodule N ⊂ M with rk_A N < ∞ is Noetherian.*
Submodules of tame modules are again tame. Furthermore,"

**Proposition 2 (4.1/2).** The direct sum of finitely many tame A-modules is a tame A-module.

*Proof.* Let M₁, M₂ be tame A-modules, and let N ⊂ M₁ ⊕ M₂ be a submodule of finite rank. We have
N ⊂ π₁(N) ⊕ π₂(N), where πᵢ denotes the projection from M₁ ⊕ M₂ to Mᵢ, i = 1, 2. Clearly,
rk_A πᵢ(N) ≤ rk_A N, i = 1, 2. Hence πᵢ(N) ⊂ Mᵢ is Noetherian, i = 1, 2. Therefore,
π₁(N) ⊕ π₂(N) and so N are Noetherian. ∎

**Proposition 3 (4.1/3).** Let A ⊂ A' be a pair of integral domains such that A' is a tame
A-module. Then each torsion-free tame A'-module M' (viewed as an A-module) is a tame A-module.

*Proof.* Let N be an A-submodule of M' of finite rank. Denote by N' the A'-submodule of M'
generated by N. Obviously, rk_{A'} N' ≤ rk_A N. Hence N' is a finitely generated A'-module. Since
M' is torsion-free, we can embed N' into a finitely generated free A'-module F', say
F' = ⊕₁ˢ A'. Then F' is a tame A-module, because A' is a tame A-module. Thus N, being an
A-submodule of finite rank in F', is finitely generated. ∎

**Proposition 4 (4.1/4).** Let A be Noetherian. Let M be an A-module such that, for each submodule
N ≠ 0 of finite rank, there exists an A-linear map Φ: N → A with ker Φ ≠ N (i.e., Φ ≠ 0). Then M is
tame.

*Proof.* First, M has no torsion. Let N ⊂ M be a submodule of finite rank. We set t := rk_A N, and
we shall prove by induction on t that N is Noetherian. For t = 0, we have N = 0 (since M is
torsion-free), and the assertion is trivial. Let t > 0. Then N ≠ 0, and there exists an A-linear
map Φ: N → A with ker Φ ≠ N. We extend Φ to a Q(A)-homomorphism Φ*: Q(N) → Q(A). Since Φ* ≠ 0, we
have

  rk_A ker Φ ≤ dim_{Q(A)} ker Φ* < dim_{Q(A)} Q(N) = rk_A N = t.

By the induction hypothesis, ker Φ is Noetherian. Because A is Noetherian, the ideal Φ(N) ⊂ A is
also a Noetherian A-module. From the exact sequence 0 → ker Φ → N → Φ(N) → 0, we now conclude that
N itself is a Noetherian A-module. ∎

"An A-module M is called *linearly separable* if, for each element x ≠ 0 in M, there is an A-linear
map Φ: M → A such that Φ(x) ≠ 0. Since all submodules of a linearly separable module are again
linearly separable, we can deduce from Proposition 4 the following result:"

**Corollary 5 (4.1/5).** Let A be Noetherian. Then each linearly separable A-module — in particular
each free A-module — is tame.

### 4.2 A Theorem of Dedekind, p. 184

"Let K' be an algebraic extension of K = Q(A). The *integral closure A' of A in K'* consists of all
elements of K' which are *integral over A*, i.e., which satisfy an integral equation with
coefficients in A. It is well known that A' is an A-algebra with K' as field of fractions; more
precisely, K' = A'_{A−{0}}. The integral domain A is called *normal* or *integrally closed* if A is
integrally closed in its field of fractions K. We are interested in the following question:

If K' is a finite K-module (i.e., if dim_K K' < ∞), is it true that A' is a finite A-module?

First, we state (and repeat the proof of) a well-known theorem which goes back to DEDEKIND."

**Theorem 1 (4.2/1), p. 185.** Let A be a normal Noetherian integral domain; let K' be a finite and
separable extension of K. Then A' is a finite A-module (and, in particular, a Noetherian ring).

*Proof.* Since K' is separable over K, the bilinear form K' × K' → K, (x, y) ↦ T(xy), is
non-degenerate (T := Tr_{K'/K}: K' → K denotes the trace function). Hence, for each basis
{x₁, …, xₙ} of the K-vector space K', there exists a K-basis {y₁, …, yₙ} of K' such that
T(x_μ y_ν) = δ_μν; μ, ν = 1, …, n. We may assume x₁, …, xₙ ∈ A'. Then we claim

  (*)  A' ⊂ Σ_{ν=1}^n A y_ν,

from which the assertion follows, because A is Noetherian. In order to verify (*), take a' ∈ A',
and write a' = Σ a_ν y_ν where a_ν ∈ K. We derive T(x_μ a') = Σ a_ν T(x_μ y_ν) = a_μ, μ = 1, …, n.
Since x_μ a' ∈ A' is integral over A, it follows that a_μ ∈ K is integral over A. Because A is
normal, we conclude a_μ ∈ A, μ = 1, …, n. ∎

### 4.3 Japanese rings. First criterion for Japaneseness, pp. 185–186

"The Theorem of DEDEKIND is not, in general, true for inseparable extensions. Therefore, we
introduce"

**Definition 1 (4.3/1).** An integral domain A is called *Japanese* if the integral closure of A in
any finite extension of K is always a finite A-module.

**Remark.** A Noetherian integral domain A is Japanese if the integral closure of A in each finite
quasi-Galois (= normal) extension of K is a finite A-module. This is clear, since an arbitrary
finite extension of K can always be embedded into its quasi-Galois closure, which is also finite
over K.

"Since each algebraic extension of a perfect field is separable, we immediately deduce from the
result of DEDEKIND (Theorem 4.2/1)"

**Proposition 2 (4.3/2).** Each normal Noetherian integral domain A, whose field of fractions is
perfect, is Japanese.

"The last proposition fails if we drop the assumption of perfectness. A counterexample is given by
the discrete valuation ring of F. K. SCHMIDT (cf. (1.6.2)). In this example the condition of the
following criterion is violated:"

**Proposition 3 (4.3/3) (First criterion for Japaneseness).** A normal Noetherian integral domain A
is Japanese if and only if the integral closure of A in each finite purely inseparable extension of
K is a finite A-module.

*Proof (p. 186).* By Proposition 2, we only have to show that the condition of this criterion is
sufficient for A to be Japanese when char A =: p ≠ 0. Let L be an arbitrary finite quasi-Galois
extension of K. Then there exists a field L_i between K and L such that L_i is purely inseparable
over K and L is separable over L_i. By assumption, the integral closure A_i of A in L_i is a finite
A-module. Since the integral closure A' of A in L equals the integral closure of A_i in L, it is
enough to show that A' is a finite A_i-module. Because A_i is normal and Noetherian, this follows
from DEDEKIND's Theorem 4.2/1. ∎

### 4.4 Tameness and Japaneseness, pp. 186–187

"Let K_a be an algebraic closure of the field K. We denote by A_a the integral closure of A in K_a.
The A-algebra A_a (which contains A) will be called a (universal) integral closure of A; this
algebra A_a is uniquely determined up to (non-canonical) A-algebra isomorphisms. If K' is any finite
extension of K, we may always assume K' ⊂ K_a. Then A' := A_a ∩ K' is the integral closure of A in
K'."

**Proposition 1 (4.4/1) (Second criterion for Japaneseness).** A Noetherian integral domain A is
Japanese if and only if A_a is a tame A-module.

*Proof.* (1) Let A be Japanese. Take any A-module N ⊂ A_a of finite rank. Then Q(N) ⊂ K_a is a
finite K-vector space. Therefore the field K' ⊂ K_a generated by Q(N) is a finite extension of K.
Thus A_a ∩ K' is a finite A-module by assumption. Hence N ⊂ A_a ∩ K' is a finite A-module, since A
is Noetherian.
(2) Let A_a be a tame A-module. Take any finite extension K' of K. Since
rk_A(A_a ∩ K') = [K' : K] < ∞, the integral closure A_a ∩ K' of A in K' is a finite A-module. ∎

"The proposition just proved can be strengthened considerably if, in addition, A is assumed to be
normal. Set p := char A. If p = 0, the ring A is always Japanese by Proposition 4.3/2. So assume
p ≠ 0. Then an important role is played by the subring

  A^{p⁻¹} := {x ∈ A_a; x^p ∈ A}

of A_a, which we may view as an A-submodule of A_a."

**Proposition 2 (4.4/2) (Third criterion for Japaneseness).** A Noetherian normal integral domain A
of characteristic p ≠ 0 is Japanese if and only if A^{p⁻¹} is a tame A-module.

*Proof.* (1) If A is Japanese, A_a is a tame A-module by Proposition 1. Hence A^{p⁻¹} is also tame,
as a submodule of A_a.
(2) Let A^{p⁻¹} be a tame A-module. As a first step, we prove by induction on n:

  *Each A-module A_n := {x ∈ A_a; x^{pⁿ} ∈ A} is tame, n = 1, 2, ….*

Assume A_n is tame (true by assumption for n = 1). In order to see that A_{n+1} is a tame A-module,
it is enough to prove (due to Proposition 4.1/3 with A' := A_n and M' := A_{n+1}) that
A_{n+1} ⊃ A_n is a tame A_n-module. But this is clear, since the Frobenius homomorphism
x ↦ x^{pⁿ} maps the A_n-module A_{n+1} bijectively onto the A-module A_1.
Now it can be easily seen by using Proposition 4.3/3 that A is Japanese. Let L ⊃ K be a finite
purely inseparable extension of K. Choose n ≥ 1 such that L ⊂ K_n := {x ∈ K_a; x^{pⁿ} ∈ K}. The
integral closure L ∩ A_a of A in L is an A-submodule of K_n ∩ A_a of finite rank (namely,
rk_A(L ∩ A_a) = dim_K L). If we can prove K_n ∩ A_a = A_n, the tameness of A_n will imply the
finiteness of the A-module L ∩ A_a.
The inclusion A_n ⊂ K_n ∩ A_a is obvious. Now take q ∈ K_n ∩ A_a. Then q^{pⁿ} ∈ K ∩ A_a, and
K ∩ A_a = A, since A is normal. Thus we have q ∈ A_n and hence K_n ∩ A_a ⊂ A_n. ∎

"We get directly from Proposition 2 and Corollary 4.1/5"

**Proposition 3 (4.4/3).** A Noetherian normal integral domain A of characteristic p ≠ 0 is
Japanese if A^{p⁻¹} is a linearly separable A-module.

"We apply this last proposition in order to obtain the (well-known) fact that polynomial rings and
rings of formal power series are Japanese."

**Proposition 4 (4.4/4).** For each field k, the polynomial ring k[X₁, …, Xₙ], as well as the ring
k[[X₁, …, Xₙ]] of formal power series in n indeterminates, is Japanese.

*Proof.* We discuss the case A := k[[X₁, …, Xₙ]] and use the fact that A is Noetherian and
factorial, and hence normal. Therefore, it is enough to show (by Proposition 3) that, for
p := char k ≠ 0, the A-module A^{p⁻¹} is linearly separable. We have

  A^{p⁻¹} = k^{p⁻¹}[[X₁^{1/p}, …, Xₙ^{1/p}]],

and this last ring is, as an A-module, isomorphic to the direct sum of finitely many copies of the
A-module A' := k^{p⁻¹}[[X₁, …, Xₙ]] (the monomials X₁^{μ₁/p} ⋯ Xₙ^{μₙ/p}, 0 ≤ μᵢ < p, form a basis).
Hence it is sufficient to prove that A' is linearly separable. Take h ∈ A', h ≠ 0, say
h = Σ a_{ν₁…νₙ} X₁^{ν₁} ⋯ Xₙ^{νₙ}, a_{ν₁…νₙ} ∈ k^{p⁻¹}, where (ν̄₁, …, ν̄ₙ) is an index tuple such
that a_{ν̄₁…ν̄ₙ} ≠ 0. Since k^{p⁻¹} is linearly separable as a k-vector space, we can choose a
k-linear map λ': k^{p⁻¹} → k such that λ'(a_{ν̄₁…ν̄ₙ}) ≠ 0. By

  λ(Σ b_{ν₁…νₙ} X₁^{ν₁} ⋯ Xₙ^{νₙ}) := Σ λ'(b_{ν₁…νₙ}) X₁^{ν₁} ⋯ Xₙ^{νₙ} ∈ A

we extend λ' to an A-linear map λ: A' → A. Since λ(h) ≠ 0 by the choice of λ', we see that A' is
linearly separable. It is clear that the proof just given works for polynomial rings k[X₁, …, Xₙ]
as well. ∎
