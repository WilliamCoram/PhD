Abstract. We compute the eigenvalues of the first few Hecke operators
Tp (N(p) ::; 100) for all Hilbert modular forms on Q(VS) of parallel
weight 2 and level of norm less than 500. This is done by exploiting
the Jacquet-Langlands correspondence which allows us to transfer such
computations to forms on the (totally definite) Hamilton quaternion
algebra over Q(V5). We use these computations to test numerically a
conjecture of Oda on periods of Hilbert modular forms.
Résumé. Nous calculons les valeurs propres des opérateurs de Hecke
Tp (N(p) ::; 100) pour toutes les formes modulaires de Hilbert primitives
de poids (2, 2) et de niveau de norme plus petite que 500 sur Q(V5).
Pour ce faire, nous exploitons la correspondence de Jacquet-Langlands
qui permet de se ramener au calcul de formes automorphes sur l'algèbre
de quaternions de Hamilton sur Q(VS). À l'aide de deux examples,
nous illustrons la consistance de nos calculs avec la conjecture de Oda
sur les périodes de formes modulaire de Hilbert.

Thanks to the works ofWiles [57], Wiles and Taylor [53] and Breuil,
Conrad, Diamond and Taylor [7], it is now known that the Taniyama­
Shimura-Weil conjecture over Q is true; i.e., that every elliptic curve
E /Q is modular. As a by-product, this put an end to the mathematieal
saga of Fermat's Last Theorem that Ribet [43] had already proved to
be a consequence of the TSW-conjecture. Before the modularity of
all rational elliptie curves was establised, the main numerical results
supportive of this conjecture were given by the Antwerp tables [5], later
extended by Cremona [14].
Even with the substantial progress accomplished by Wiles in [57]
and Skinner-Wiles [50], the hope for a proof of the full TSW-conjecture
for elliptie curves over general number fields still seems far away. Mean­
while, motivated by Wiles [57] and related works, Darmon [15] has
proposed a strategy to tackle the generalized Fermat Last Theorem.
This strategy requires a very good understanding of Hilbert modular
forms over the real subfields of certain cyclotomie fields and consists of
a series of conjectures. So numerical investigations in order to gather
more data supportive of Shimura-Tanyama-Weil conjecture over gen­
eral number fields still has a role to play as it did in the rational case.
The main ingredient in Cremona [14] is the notion of modular sym­
bols that gives a very concrete description of the first Homology group

The goal of this thesis was two fold:first discuss an alternative
way of computing Hilbert modular newforms of weight 2 on certain
quadratic fields and second, see if the forms obtained could provide

more as a work in progress, and the few results we could obtain merely
illustrate the mystery of the Oda conjecture.

8. Conclusion.
The theory of algebraic modular forms in Gross [29] gives a very
nice way to compute Hilbert modular eigenforms of weight 2 over F =
Q(J5) by transferring such computations to the Hamilton quaternion
algebra by means of the Jacquet-Langlands correspondence. The same
approach can be used over any totally real field F (of narrow class
number one) and for more general (including non-parrallel) weights (see
the recent paper ofConsani and Scholten [12]). The major requirement
of the algorithms we have used is finding an efficient way to compute
the Hecke operators. This becomes an imperative issue as the norm
of the level increases, in order to get a satisfactory precision on the
period moduli of the computed newforms. So it would be interesting
to see wether or not the icosian ring admits a Euclidean algorithm. If
affirmative, such a result will have the dramatic effect of increasing the
efficiency of our algorithm. This is an issue that we intend to explore.
In contrast to the one dimension al case, the computation of periods
in higher dimension seems to be a delicate matter. Indeed, from the
examples we discussed, the computation of the Oda periods requires a
large number of Hecke eigenvalues. Indeed, for the two smallest primes
in our list, namely 31 and 41, we needed the Hecke eigenvalues for all
primes of norm less than or equal to 10000. But this could only give
us an accuracy of no more than 20 decimalsj and this does not seem
to he enough to determine either an integral or a rational model in the
corresponding isogeny classes. The main difficulty lies in the fact that
the computation of the Oda periods implicitly involves the choice of a
lattice in the rational cohomology group H 2 (Mr , Q) associated to the
===== PDF PAGE 141 =====
8. CONCLUSION. 133
newform f, and this is a big difference with the one dimensional case.
Indeed, when F = Q, not only is it known that the elliptic curve Er
is rational, but also a result of Edixhoven [21] on the Manin constant,
says that the modular symbols computations, as done in Cremona [14],
directly leads to an integral model. This greatly cuts down the amount
of computations needed in this case. The modular parametrization of
elliptic curves play a rôle in the definition of the Manin constant. In
our setting, one is then tempted to consider its natural analogue which
is the parametrization of elliptic curves by Shimura curves. Due to
the lack of q-expansion in this situation, one then asks for the right
substitute to the Manin constant and how this will ensure us with a
good model for the isogeny class of the elliptic curve corresponding to
our newform. There is no doubt that this question is related to the one
of accessing the fundamental periods of the newform f. As this is a
very active field in the theory of Hilbert modular forms, the few results
in our second chapter should be seen more as a work in progress. We
would like to explore the issue of overcoming the torsion in H 2 (Mr, Q)
in order to obtain an integral model for the computed isogeny class.
We would also like to extend the PARI search that we started with the
help of Prof. N. Elkies and see if we can make use of results in the
spirit of Faltings-Serre or the more recent results of Skinner-Wiles to
prove the modularity of the curves we have obtained (and will obtain).
